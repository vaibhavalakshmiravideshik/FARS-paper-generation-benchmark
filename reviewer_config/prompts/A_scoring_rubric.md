Review Scoring Rubrics

Quality (1-4)
Is the submission technically sound? Are claims well supported?
4 (excellent): Technically flawless, claims fully supported by theory or experiments, methods appropriate and complete.
3 (good): Technically solid with minor issues, claims mostly supported, methods appropriate.
2 (fair): Some technical concerns, claims partially supported, methodology has gaps.
1 (poor): Significant technical flaws, claims unsupported, inappropriate methods.

Clarity (1-4)
Is the submission clearly written and well organized?
4 (excellent): Superbly written, provides enough information for expert reproduction.
3 (good): Well written and organized, minor improvements possible.
2 (fair): Understandable but needs improvement in organization or clarity.
1 (poor): Poorly written, difficult to follow, missing critical information.

Significance (1-4)
Are the results impactful? Will others build on this work?
4 (excellent): Groundbreaking impact, addresses a critical problem substantially better than prior work.
3 (good): Important contribution, others will likely use or build on the ideas.
2 (fair): Modest contribution, limited impact on the field.
1 (poor): Minimal significance, does not advance understanding.

Originality (1-4)
Does the work provide new insights or novel methods?
4 (excellent): Highly original, introduces fundamentally new ideas or approaches.
3 (good): Novel contribution, clear differentiation from prior work with good justification.
2 (fair): Some novelty but largely incremental over existing work.
1 (poor): Little originality, mostly reproduces known results.

Overall Score (1-6)
6 (Strong Accept): Technically flawless with groundbreaking impact, exceptionally strong evaluation, no unaddressed ethical concerns.
5 (Accept): Technically solid, high impact on at least one sub-area of the field, good-to-excellent evaluation.
4 (Borderline Accept): Technically solid, reasons to accept outweigh reasons to reject.
3 (Borderline Reject): Technically solid, reasons to reject outweigh reasons to accept.
2 (Reject): Technical flaws, weak evaluation, inadequate reproducibility.
1 (Strong Reject): Already well-known results or unaddressed ethical considerations.

Confidence (1-5)
5: Absolutely certain. Very familiar with related work, checked math/details carefully.
4: Confident but not absolutely certain. Unlikely you missed something.
3: Fairly confident. Possible you missed some parts or related work.
2: Willing to defend but may have missed central parts.
1: Educated guess. Not in your area or submission was difficult to understand.
